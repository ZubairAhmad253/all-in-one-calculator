Body mass index (BMI) is the most widely used check of whether your weight is in a healthy range. It's quick, free and useful, but it's also easy to over-read. Here's what the number means and when to look beyond it.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. Talk to a doctor about your own health.*

## How BMI is calculated

**BMI = weight in kilograms ÷ (height in metres)²**

In imperial units: **BMI = 703 × weight in pounds ÷ (height in inches)²**

For someone who weighs **70 kg** and is **1.75 m** tall:

70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75) = 70 ÷ 3.0625 = **22.9**

## BMI categories for adults

The World Health Organization uses these ranges for adults:

| BMI | Category |
| --- | --- |
| Under 18.5 | Underweight |
| 18.5 to 24.9 | Healthy weight |
| 25.0 to 29.9 | Overweight |
| 30.0 and above | Obesity |

For a person 1.75 m tall, a healthy BMI means a weight between about **56.7 kg and 76.3 kg**. That's a wide range, because healthy bodies come in many shapes.

## What BMI is good for

BMI is useful because it's simple and consistent. Across large groups of people, a higher BMI is linked to higher risks of type 2 diabetes, high blood pressure, heart disease and some cancers. That makes it a good **screening tool**: a quick first check that can prompt a closer look.

## What BMI misses

BMI only uses height and weight, so it can't see *what* that weight is made of or *where* it sits.

### Muscle vs fat

Muscle is denser than fat. A muscular athlete who is 1.88 m tall and weighs 95 kg has a BMI of **26.9**, officially "overweight", despite having little body fat.

### Where fat is stored

Fat around the waist (visceral fat) carries more health risk than fat on the hips and thighs. Two people with the same BMI can have very different risks.

### Age

Older adults naturally lose muscle, so a "healthy" BMI can hide a higher body-fat percentage. Some research suggests a slightly higher BMI may be fine, or even protective, in later life.

### Ethnicity

Health risks can rise at a lower BMI in people of South Asian, Chinese and some other Asian backgrounds. For these groups, many health bodies suggest treating **23 or more as overweight** and **27.5 or more as obesity**.

### Children and pregnancy

Adult BMI categories don't apply to children, who are compared against growth charts for their age and sex. BMI isn't used to judge weight during pregnancy either.

## Two simple extra checks

### Waist-to-height ratio

Measure your waist (halfway between your lowest rib and your hip bone) and divide it by your height, in the same units. A widely used guide is to **keep your waist to less than half your height**, a ratio under 0.5.

For someone 175 cm tall, that means a waist under about **88 cm**.

### Waist size

Many guidelines treat a waist over **94 cm (37 in) for men** or **80 cm (31.5 in) for women** as a sign of higher risk, though thresholds vary by ethnicity.

## If your BMI is outside the healthy range

- **Don't panic.** BMI is one number, not a diagnosis.
- **Look at the bigger picture:** waist size, blood pressure, activity, diet, sleep and family history all matter.
- **Aim for gradual change:** losing 5–10% of your body weight, if you're above the healthy range, can bring real health benefits.
- **Talk to a doctor,** especially if you're underweight, have lost weight without trying, or have other health conditions.

## Common questions

### Is BMI different for men and women?

The adult BMI categories are the same for men and women. At the same BMI, however, women on average carry more body fat than men, which is one reason BMI works best alongside other measures like waist size.

### I'm very muscular. Should I ignore my BMI?

If you train regularly and your BMI is in the overweight range mainly because of muscle, your health risk may not be raised. Waist-to-height ratio is a better second check for you: if your waist is under half your height, a slightly high BMI is less of a concern.

### How often should I check my BMI?

For most adults, checking every few months is plenty. Body weight naturally changes by 1–2 kg from day to day with water and food, so focus on the trend over weeks rather than daily changes.

## Key takeaways

- A BMI of 18.5 to 24.9 is considered a healthy weight for most adults.
- BMI can't tell muscle from fat or show where fat is stored.
- Waist-to-height ratio is a useful second check: keep your waist under half your height.
