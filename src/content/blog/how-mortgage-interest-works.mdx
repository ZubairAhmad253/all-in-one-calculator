Your mortgage payment is the same every month, but what it pays for changes a lot over time. Understanding that split is the key to paying less interest, sometimes a lot less.

This guide uses one example throughout: a **$320,000 loan at 6.5% for 30 years**. You can follow along in the [mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator).

## Where your monthly payment goes

A fixed-rate mortgage payment covers two things:

- **Interest:** what the lender charges for the money you still owe.
- **Principal:** the part that actually pays down the loan.

For our example, the payment is **$2,022.62 a month** for principal and interest. (Property tax and insurance usually come on top, but they don't affect this split.)

## Why early payments are mostly interest

Each month, interest is charged on the balance you still owe. The rate is 6.5% a year, so the monthly rate is 6.5 ÷ 12 ≈ 0.54%.

In the very first month, you owe the full $320,000:

- Interest: $320,000 × 0.065 ÷ 12 = **$1,733.33**
- Principal: $2,022.62 − $1,733.33 = **$289.28**

So about **86% of your first payment is interest**. Over the whole first year you pay about **$20,695 in interest** and only **$3,577 off the loan**.

As the balance slowly falls, each month's interest charge gets a little smaller, so a little more of the same payment goes to principal. The balance falls slowly at first and quickly at the end. By the last few years, almost the whole payment is principal.

## The true cost over 30 years

Over 360 payments you pay about **$728,142** in total. That is the $320,000 you borrowed plus **$408,142 in interest**, more than the loan itself.

That number is why the choices below are worth thinking about.

## How extra payments cut the cost

Any extra you pay goes straight to principal. That lowers the balance, which lowers every future interest charge, which means more of each later payment goes to principal too. The effect compounds in your favour.

Here is what paying a little extra each month does to our example:

| Extra each month | Paid off in | Interest saved | Time saved |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $100 | 26 years 2 months | $61,698 | 3 years 10 months |
| $200 | 23 years 5 months | $105,429 | 6 years 7 months |
| $500 | 18 years | $185,552 | 12 years |

An extra $200 a month, about 10% more than the regular payment, saves over **$105,000** and takes more than six years off the loan.

### The "13th payment" trick

Some people make one extra full payment a year, or pay half the monthly amount every two weeks, which adds up to the same thing. For our example, that works out to about $168.55 extra a month. It clears the loan in **24 years 2 months** and saves about **$93,073** in interest.

## Shorter term or extra payments?

A 15-year mortgage for the same $320,000 at 6.5% costs **$2,787.54 a month**, about $765 more, but the total interest drops to **$181,758**. That's roughly $226,000 less than the 30-year loan. In practice, 15-year loans often come with a lower rate too, which saves even more.

The trade-off:

- **A 15-year loan** forces the savings, but the higher payment is fixed even in a tight month.
- **A 30-year loan plus voluntary extra payments** gives you flexibility: pay extra when you can, stop when you need to.

If you choose the second route, set the extra payment up as an automatic transfer so it actually happens.

## Rate matters more than you think

Dropping the rate from 6.5% to **6.0%** lowers the payment to $1,918.56 and cuts total interest to **$370,682**. That's about **$37,000 saved** from half a percentage point. Shopping around lenders, improving your credit score and paying points can all be worth real money.

## Before you pay extra

- **Check for prepayment penalties.** Most mortgages allow extra payments, but confirm first.
- **Ask for extra payments to go to principal.** Some lenders apply them to next month's payment instead.
- **Build an emergency fund first.** Money in your mortgage is hard to get back out quickly.
- **Compare with other uses.** Paying off high-interest debt, like credit cards, usually comes first.

## Common questions

### Is it better to pay extra on my mortgage or invest the money?

It depends on your rate and your comfort with risk. Paying extra on a 6.5% mortgage is like earning a guaranteed 6.5% return, with no risk at all. Long-term stock market returns have often been higher than that, but they go up and down. Many people split the difference: they build an emergency fund, take any employer retirement match, and then divide spare money between extra mortgage payments and investing.

### Does an extra payment early in the loan save more than one later?

Yes. An extra payment in year one stops interest building on that money for the remaining 29 years. The same payment in year 25 only saves interest for five years. If you can only afford a few extra payments, make them as early as possible.

### Should I refinance to a lower rate?

Compare the monthly saving with the closing costs. If refinancing saves $150 a month and costs $4,500, it takes 30 months to break even. It's usually worth it if you'll stay in the home well beyond that point. Be careful about resetting to a new 30-year term, which can increase the total interest even at a lower rate.

## Key takeaways

- Early payments are mostly interest because interest is charged on the full balance.
- Over 30 years, interest can cost more than the home loan itself.
- Small, regular extra payments save tens of thousands and years of payments.
- A lower rate or shorter term changes the total cost dramatically.
