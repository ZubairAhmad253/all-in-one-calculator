"Eat 2,000 calories a day" is printed on food labels everywhere, but your real number could be several hundred calories higher or lower. Here's how to work out your own, and how to use it.

*This article is general information, not medical advice. If you have a health condition, are pregnant or breastfeeding, speak to a doctor or dietitian.*

## Two numbers you need to know

### BMR: your basal metabolic rate

Your BMR is the energy your body uses **at complete rest**, just to keep your heart beating, lungs breathing and brain working. For most people it's the biggest part of their daily calorie use.

### TDEE: your total daily energy expenditure

Your TDEE is **everything you burn in a day**: your BMR plus walking, working, exercising and even digesting food. This is the number that matters for your weight.

## How BMR is calculated

The **Mifflin–St Jeor equation** is the most widely recommended formula for adults:

- **Men:** BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age + 5
- **Women:** BMR = 10 × weight (kg) + 6.25 × height (cm) − 5 × age − 161

### Example

A **30-year-old man**, **80 kg** and **180 cm** tall:

10 × 80 + 6.25 × 180 − 5 × 30 + 5 = 800 + 1,125 − 150 + 5 = **1,780 kcal a day**

## From BMR to TDEE

Multiply your BMR by an activity factor:

| Activity level | Factor | Our example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sedentary (desk job, little exercise) | 1.2 | 2,136 kcal |
| Lightly active (exercise 1–3 days a week) | 1.375 | 2,448 kcal |
| Moderately active (3–5 days a week) | 1.55 | 2,759 kcal |
| Very active (6–7 days a week) | 1.725 | 3,071 kcal |

The difference between "sedentary" and "moderately active" is over 600 calories a day for the same person. **Most people overestimate their activity**, so if you're unsure, pick the lower level.

## How to lose, keep or gain weight

A kilogram of body fat stores roughly **7,700 kcal** (about 3,500 kcal per pound). So:

- To lose about **0.5 kg a week**, eat about **550 kcal a day less** than your TDEE.
- To gain about **0.25 kg a week** (for building muscle), eat about **275 kcal a day more**.

### A second example

A **35-year-old woman**, **65 kg** and **165 cm**, who exercises lightly:

- BMR: 10 × 65 + 6.25 × 165 − 5 × 35 − 161 = **1,345 kcal**
- TDEE: 1,345 × 1.375 ≈ **1,850 kcal**
- To lose 0.5 kg a week: 1,850 − 550 ≈ **1,300 kcal a day**

That's close to the level many guidelines treat as a minimum for women (around 1,200 kcal), so a gentler target of about 0.25 kg a week (around 1,575 kcal) may be easier to sustain.

## Why the numbers are only a starting point

- **Formulas are averages.** Your real needs could be about 10% different.
- **Your body adapts.** As you lose weight, your TDEE falls, so recalculate every few kilos.
- **Food labels and trackers aren't exact** either.

The best approach is to use the calculated figure for **two to three weeks**, watch what your weight actually does, and adjust by 100–200 kcal if needed.

## Tips that make a calorie target easier

- **Prioritise protein:** it's filling and helps protect muscle while losing weight.
- **Fill up on fibre:** vegetables, fruit, beans and whole grains.
- **Watch drinks:** juice, sugary coffee drinks and alcohol add up quickly.
- **Stay active:** walking more raises your TDEE without needing to eat less.
- **Sleep well:** poor sleep makes hunger harder to manage.

## Common questions

### Do I need to count calories to lose weight?

Not necessarily. Calorie counting is one tool, and many people lose weight by changing habits instead: smaller portions, fewer sugary drinks, more vegetables and protein, and more walking. Knowing your rough TDEE is still useful as a reality check.

### Should I eat back the calories I burn exercising?

If you chose an activity level that already includes your workouts, no, because they're already counted. Fitness trackers also tend to overestimate calories burned, so if you do eat some back, start with about half.

### Why have I stopped losing weight?

As you lose weight, your body needs fewer calories, and it may also adapt by becoming a little more efficient. Recalculate your needs at your new weight, check that portions haven't crept up, and give it a few weeks: changes in water weight can hide fat loss for a while.

## Key takeaways

- BMR is what you burn at rest; TDEE adds all your daily activity.
- Mifflin–St Jeor is the most reliable everyday formula.
- About 550 kcal a day below your TDEE loses about 0.5 kg a week.
- Treat the result as a starting point and adjust based on real progress.
