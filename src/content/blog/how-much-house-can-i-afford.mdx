Your lender's pre-approval tells you how much you *can* borrow. It doesn't tell you how much you *should* borrow. This guide shows you how to work out a comfortable budget.

## The 28/36 rule

Most lenders start with two ratios, based on your **gross monthly income** (your income before tax):

- **28%:** your total housing cost (mortgage, property tax, insurance and HOA fees) should be at most 28% of your gross monthly income.
- **36%:** all your debt payments together, including car loans, student loans and credit cards, should be at most 36%.

| Gross annual income | Max housing cost (28%) | Max total debt (36%) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $60,000 | $1,400 / month | $1,800 / month |
| $90,000 | $2,100 / month | $2,700 / month |
| $120,000 | $2,800 / month | $3,600 / month |

## Turning a monthly budget into a home price

Once you know your maximum monthly payment, work backwards:

1. Take off estimated property tax, insurance and HOA fees. What's left is your budget for **principal and interest**.
2. Use the interest rate and loan term you expect to get to find the loan amount that payment covers.
3. Add your down payment to get your price range.

The quickest way is to open the [mortgage calculator](/mortgage-calculator) and adjust the home price until the monthly payment matches your budget.

## Costs people forget

- **Closing costs:** usually 2 to 5% of the loan amount, paid when you buy.
- **Maintenance:** a common rule of thumb is 1% of the home's value each year.
- **Emergency fund:** keep three to six months of expenses saved *after* your down payment.

## Key takeaways

- Keep total housing costs under about 28% of your gross income.
- Include taxes, insurance and fees, not just the loan payment.
- Budget for closing costs and maintenance before you choose a price.
